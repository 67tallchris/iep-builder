# Behavior Guide: Special Factor, FBA, BSP/BIP, and Crisis Planning

Sources: AZ-TAS 2025 (ADE), Center on PBIS *Basic FBA to BSP Trainer's Manual* and *Tier 3 Brief
FBA Guide*, A.R.S. §15-105, ADE MTBS training materials.

---

## 1. Behavior is a required special-factor consideration

When behavior impedes the student's learning or that of others, the IEP team must consider the
behavior, develop **positive behavioral interventions and supports**, and identify how progress
will be monitored (AzTAS 2025 p. 20; 34 C.F.R. §300.324(a)(2)(i)).

What this means for writing:
- The PLAAFP must describe the behavior in observable terms with baseline data.
- The IEP must include positive supports — not merely consequences.
- At least one goal (or a documented BIP with its own monitoring) must track it.
- Staff supports (training, consultation, plans) should be listed where needed.

---

## 2. FBA — Functional Behavioral Assessment

**What it is:** A systematic process to identify the function (purpose) behavior serves for the
student, so supports address the cause rather than the symptom.

**When to conduct (Arizona-relevant triggers):**
- Behavior impedes learning or the learning of others (team decision).
- Disciplinary removal that constitutes a change of placement (IDEA manifestation review); if no
  FBA/BIP exists, one must be conducted.
- **Arizona:** repeated use of restraint or seclusion during a school year triggers a review that
  includes whether the pupil requires an FBA (A.R.S. §15-105(D)(3)).
- Best practice: before writing any behavioral goal beyond universal supports.

**Basic FBA steps (PBIS mild/moderate model):**
1. **Define the behavior operationally** — observable, measurable, countable.
   - Weak: "is defiant." Strong: "tears task materials or pushes them off the desk."
2. **Indirect assessment** — teacher/parent/student interviews, rating scales, record review.
3. **Direct observation** — ABC data (antecedent, behavior, consequence), scatterplots,
   duration/frequency counts across settings and times.
4. **Summarize patterns** — settings, times, people, tasks, and consequences that predict and
   follow the behavior.
5. **Hypothesize function** — typically one or more of:
   | Function | Looks like | Matched supports |
   |---|---|---|
   | Access to attention (adult/peer) | Behavior reliable when staff busy | Scheduled attention, peer tutoring, class jobs |
   | Escape/avoidance (task, demand, setting) | Behavior starts with difficult work | Task chunking, choice, pre-teaching, breaks |
   | Access to tangibles/activity | Behavior around preferred items | Visual schedules, first/then, earned access |
   | Sensory/automatic | Occurs across contexts, self-stimulating | Sensory breaks, fidget/regulation tools, movement |

6. **Build the BSP from the hypothesis** (see below), then monitor and revise.

## 3. BSP/BIP — Behavior Support Plan components

A complete behavior support plan (attach to or embed in the IEP) includes:

1. **Target behavior(s)** — operational definition(s) with baseline data.
2. **Hypothesized function** — stated in plain language.
3. **Antecedent/prevention strategies** — what changes in the environment, task, or schedule.
4. **Replacement behavior instruction** — the new skill taught to serve the same function
   (e.g., "raise hand and ask for a break" instead of "leave the room").
5. **Consequence strategies** — how staff respond to replacement behavior and to the target
   behavior, consistently and calmly, including reinforcement systems.
6. **Crisis/safety plan** — steps if behavior escalates, who does what, de-escalation first,
   and (if required) restraint/seclusion protocols under A.R.S. §15-105.
7. **Data collection** — who records what, when, and how data are reviewed.
8. **Staff responsibilities** — roles, training, and fidelity checks.
9. **Review schedule** — how often the team reviews data and updates the plan.

## 4. Worked BIP skeleton (fictional, mild/moderate)

**Target behavior:** During independent writing (3x/week, 30 min), Student tears or throws
assignment papers, an average of 2.4 times per session (baseline: 12 of 15 sessions observed).

**Function (hypothesis):** Escape from writing tasks that feel overwhelming and effortful
(antecedent: multi-paragraph prompts; consequence: task removed or reduced).

**Prevention:**
- Break writing into 2–3 sentence chunks with a visual checklist; student checks off each chunk.
- Provide sentence starters and a word bank; pre-teach vocabulary before writing.
- Offer a choice of topic and of writing tool (paper, tablet, speech-to-text).
- Timed "first 5 minutes together" with teacher check-in.

**Replacement behavior instruction:**
- Teach and practice "I need a break" card + 2-minute break with a timer; role-play weekly.
- Teach a 3-step error-recovery routine ("skip it, come back, ask for help").

**Consequences:**
- Immediate specific praise and a point toward a classroom reinforcement menu when the
  replacement behavior is used.
- If materials are torn: neutral redirection to the chunked task after a 2-minute reset; do not
  remove the writing task for the day (avoid reinforcing escape).

**Crisis plan:** If escalation continues, use de-escalation script, move peers to the side,
offer choice of break locations; restraint/seclusion only if imminent danger and less-restrictive
interventions insufficient, with same-day parent notice (A.R.S. §15-105).

**Data:** Daily frequency count of target and replacement behavior; weekly review by teacher +
case manager; monthly team check.

## 5. Behavior goals and progress monitoring

- Write goals about the **replacement skill**, not only the reduction of the problem behavior.
- Baseline and target must use the same measurement system (e.g., opportunities per day, % of
  intervals, frequency per class period).
- Common measures: event/frequency counts, duration, latency, partial-interval recording,
  daily behavior report cards (DBRC), check-in/check-out (CICO) points.
- Include a plan for **fading** supports as the student becomes independent.
- Review data at least monthly; reconvene the team if progress is not as expected
  (annual review or sooner if progress substantially deviates — AzTAS 2025 p. 23).

### Check-in/Check-out (CICO) snapshot
- Morning check-in with a mentor; set 2–3 behavior goals.
- Teacher rating card each period; points tied to reinforcement menu.
- Afternoon check-out with mentor; home report.
- Best for students who respond to adult attention and need frequent feedback (Tier 2).

## 6. Discipline, manifestation, and restraint/seclusion notes

- IDEA discipline protections apply after 10 cumulative days of removal in a school year;
  a manifestation determination review asks whether the conduct was caused by/related to the
  disability or a failure to implement the IEP. A BIP/FBA is central evidence.
- **Arizona restraint/seclusion (A.R.S. §15-105):**
  - Only when behavior presents **imminent danger of bodily harm** and less-restrictive
    interventions appear insufficient.
  - Continuous visual monitoring; ends when danger ends; trained staff; no breathing
    obstruction; proportionate to age/condition.
  - Same-day parent notice (within 24 hours if same-day is impossible) plus written
    documentation of triggers, behavior/precursors, technique, and duration.
  - Repeated use in a year → review strategies and whether an FBA is needed.
  - Law-enforcement summons instead of restraint follows the same reporting/review.
  - Seclusion does **not** include a voluntary timeout/calming separation written into the IEP,
    BIP, or safety plan.

**Tool implications:**
- A dedicated "Behavior" workspace that generates the BSP skeleton above and links it to goals.
- A safety-plan template that distinguishes proactive supports from emergency procedures and
  includes the A.R.S. §15-105 notice/documentation checklist.
- Reminders in the UI: positive supports first; restraint/seclusion is emergency-only.

## 7. Arizona pathways for behavior-heavy needs

- **MTSS/MTBS:** document Tier 1/2/3 interventions before referral where appropriate.
- **ED eligibility:** requires that the behavior/emotional condition adversely affects educational
  performance and is not solely a social maladjustment; evaluation data should show patterns
  across settings and time.
- **OHI/ADHD:** behavior supports are often executive-function supports (schedules, timers,
  chunking, movement breaks, organizational routines) rather than contingency plans.
- **Counseling** can be a related service when needed to benefit from special education.
- **FIEP (Facilitated IEP):** Arizona's voluntary process when a behavior-heavy case is likely to
  be contentious.
